# Lessons

One-line rules distilled from many review rounds. Use them to anticipate feedback instead of relearning it.

1. Boring is the only real failure. Every beat must be funny, cool or something people would care about, or it goes.
2. Open on the best result, not the setup. A greeting, a logo or a held still in the first seconds loses the viewer.
3. Ask for the hook direction before building it. A flashy hook the user didn't want costs a full round.
4. Keep the hook to its payload words: no setup filler, no repeated clause, no stammer. A slow hook can't keep up with the
   text popping up.
5. Show the whole named thing. If the line names a button, a map or a diagram, frame all of it, with a focus box. A crop
   that hides it reads as a mistake.
6. Match the picture to the line. When the speaker describes an action, show that exact moment, not a prettier one.
7. Cut every um, uh and restart, in every scene. The transcript looks clean because transcribers drop them: run
   `find_ums.py` on the stem and on the final.
8. Cut only inside a measured pause. A cut mid-word, or on a faint murmur after a word, is audible.
9. Transcribe the final's first and last seconds. A clipped first word or a sign-off cut on its last syllable is the most
   common complaint.
10. Fixed gain, then a limiter. Adaptive loudness normalisation pumps; a compressor `makeup` is linear, not dB.
11. One music track across a set, and only one the user supplied or approved. Never pick a song on your own.
12. No line heard twice. Hook lines come out of the body.
13. Hand-offs never double-expose. A beat is gone before the next rises in the same place; step words never swap in place.
14. Key lines hold into their breath and melt. Snapping away right after the important line reads as an abrupt jump.
15. Captions: one line, centred on the layout's axis, broken by sentence and comma, never ending on a weak word. Look at a
    real frame before calling them done.
16. Subtext, labels and tooltips all speak in one voice and one style. Small inconsistencies get noticed.
17. Size every card and caption for a phone. A quiet, tiny or grey title that holds reads as a stalled transition.
18. Never crop a before/after for portrait. Stack both whole.
19. Footage plays at 1x in one continuous stretch, at 30 fps. Loops, montages and 24/25 fps sources read as choppy.
20. Measure margins on the rendered frame. A layout that looked right in code can run off the edge.
21. Every label and number is literally true to the source. Quote real prompts and real names; never approximate.
22. A reframe or re-cut changes every film-time file. Cut the voice, the video and any second video together, then check
    sync on frames.
23. Look at the render as a composition before shipping, not just against the note. "It follows the instruction" is not
    "it looks right".
24. "Vertical" and other format words map to the kit's own presets. Ask which size, then reuse the preset; never invent a
    new layout.
25. Variation is expected. Reuse a layout the user liked, but don't clone the last film.
